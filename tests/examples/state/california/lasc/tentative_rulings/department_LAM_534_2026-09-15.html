DEPARTMENT 534 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 30-2026-01564595    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 534

Background

 

On April 24, 2026, Plaintiff Rolan Bigalimov filed this action in the Orange County Superior Court against Judge Carmen Luege arising out of an underlying defamation case brought by Bigalimov. The complaint asserts enumerated causes of action for: (1) fraud (denial of ex parte for continuance of trial); (2) fraud (denial of 1st Peremptory Challenge); (3) fraud (denial of 2nd Peremptory Challenge); (4) perjury – false order denying 2nd Peremptory Challenge; (5) fraud – alteration of court’s records; (6) intentional infliction of emotional distress for 1st cause of action; (7) intentional infliction of emotional distress for 2nd cause of action; (8) intentional infliction of emotional distress for 3rd cause of action; and (9) intentional infliction of emotional distress for 5th cause of action. The complaint seeks $10,000,000.00 in damages.

 

On May 4, 2026, this case was ordered reassigned to this Court for all purposes.

 

Demurrer

 

Defendant Luege demurs to the complaint and each cause of action therein on the grounds that: (1) the Court lacks subject matter jurisdiction to grant the relief sought; (2) The complaint is barred by the doctrine of absolute judicial immunity; (3) The complaint is barred for failure to comply with the California Government Claims Act; and (4) The complaint does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action.

 

Discussion

 

Standard

 

When ruling on a demurrer, the Court accepts the truth of all properly pleaded material facts of the subject pleading, (Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 966-967), and draws reasonable “inferences favorable to the plaintiff, not the defendant.” (Perez v. Golden Empire Transit Dist. (2012) 209 Cal.App.4th 1228, 1239.) A “demurrer does not, however, admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law alleged in the pleading, or the construction of instruments pleaded, or facts impossible in law.” (S. Shore Land Co. v. Petersen (1964) 226 Cal.App.2d 725, 732.) Courts may also consider matters properly subject to judicial notice, (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318), but need not accept “contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law.” (Daar v. Yellow Cab Co. (1967) 67 Cal. 2d 695, 71.)

 

If the demurrer is sustained, plaintiff must prove the possibility of cure by amendment. (Czajkowski v. Haskell & White, LLP (2012) 208 Cal.App.4th 166, 173 (citing Grinzi v. San Diego Hospice Corp. (2004) 120 Cal.App.4th 72, 78-79).) 

 

Plaintiff Does Not Seek Declaratory or Injunctive Relief

 

In the demurrer, Defendant contends Plaintiff “seeks declaratory and injunctive relief from Judge Luege’s orders denying his preemptory challenges and ex parte application to continue trial . . . The complaint also requests non-specific nonmonetary, declaratory or injunctive relief” to overturn, reverse, or modify Judge Luege’s rulings. Defendant contends this Court lacks jurisdiction to provide such relief, relying upon authority that one department of a superior court cannot overrule or otherwise interfere with another. (See e.g. Glade v. Glade (1995) 38 Cal.App.4th 1441, 145 (“[o]ne department of the superior court cannot enjoin, restrain, or otherwise interfere with the judicial act of another department of the superior court. . . . A judgment rendered in one department of the superior court is binding on that matter upon all other departments until such time as the judgment is overturned.”).) In reply, Defendant cites allegations in the complaint where Plaintiff requests the court “grant relief,” and presumes such relief is nonmonetary.

 

However, as noted by Plaintiff in opposition, the complaint does not seek declaratory or injunctive relief from the orders, only monetary damages. (Opp. at 5:25 (“I, simply, have not asked the Court ‘to reverse or modify’ orders of Hon. Luege.”). See also Compl. at 44:9-10.)

 

Defendant’s jurisdictional argument does not provide a basis to sustain the demurrer.

 

Plaintiff’s Complaint and All Causes of Action Therein are Barred by Judicial Immunity

 

Each of Plaintiff’s causes of action seek monetary relief against a judicial officer for decisions made in their official capacity. As such, each cause of action is barred by judicial immunity. (See e.g. Tagliavia v. County of Los Angeles (1980) 112 Cal.App.3d 759, 761 (“The decisions of this state uniformly and consistently grant immunity from civil suit to judges in the exercise of their judicial functions. [Citation] That is true even if the acts are in excess of the jurisdiction of the judge and are alleged to have been done maliciously and corruptly.”); Soliz v. Williams (1999) 74 Cal.App.4th 577, 586 (“Judicial immunity from a civil action for monetary damages is absolute.”).)

 

Plaintiff’s claims of error regarding the propriety of Judge Luege’s rulings were for the Court of Appeal, not a basis for personal liability. (See e.g. Forrester v. White (1988) 484 U.S. 219, 227, superseded by statute on another ground in Leclerc v. Webb (2003) 270 F.Supp.2d 779, 792-793 (“Most judicial mistakes or wrongs are open to correction through ordinary mechanisms of review, which are largely free of the harmful side-effects inevitably associated with exposing judges to personal liability.”).)

 

The demurrer is therefore SUSTAINED without leave to amend as to the entire complaint and all causes of action asserted therein. (See Frost v. Geernaert (1988) 200 Cal.App.3d 1104, 1109–1110 (“the trial court did not err in granting defendants’ demurrer without leave to amend and dismissing the action against them; under the substantive law, no liability exists.”).)

 

Plaintiff’s Complaint Alleges Compliance with the Tort Claims Act

 

Defendant argues “Plaintiff did not allege facts in the complaint to demonstrate that he timely complied with the government claim presentation requirements. Accordingly, the demurrer must be sustained without leave to amend.” (Dem. at 7:5-7.) In opposition, Plaintiff argues they did not need to file a government claim as a prerequisite to bringing suit.

 

Defendant is a public employee and each of Plaintiff’s claims arise out of the performance of Defendant’s duties as a judicial officer. Accordingly, Plaintiff was required to allege compliance with the Tort Claims Act. (Watson v. State of California (1993) 21 Cal.App.4th 836, 843 (“It is well settled that a government claim must be filed with the public entity before a tort action is brought against the public entity or public employee.”); Olson v. Manhattan Beach Unified School Dist. (2017) 17 Cal.App.5th 1052, 1055 n.1 (“The defense of noncompliance with the Government Claims Act also applies to the claims against Matthews. (See Gov. Code, § 950.2 [‘a cause of action against a public employee or former public employee for injury resulting from an act or omission in the scope of his employment as a public employee is barred if an action against the employing public entity for such injury is barred....’].).”); Wood v. Riverside General Hospital (1994) 25 Cal.App.4th 1113, 1119 (“compliance with the claims statute is an element of plaintiff's cause of action.”).)

 

However, Plaintiff’s complaint consists of a Judicial Council form complaint with an attached typewritten pleading. The Judicial Council form complaint alleges compliance. (Compl. at PLD-PI-001 at p. 2 ¶ 9(a).) The Court must accept this allegation as true at the demurrer stage. (Esparza v. Kaweah Delta Dist. Hospital (2016) 3 Cal.App.5th 547, 553 (“the ultimate fact of compliance with the claims presentation requirement in the Government Claims Act can be pled using a general allegation.”).)

 

Accordingly, Defendant’s claim presentation argument does not provide a separate basis to sustain the demurrer.

 

Plaintiff’s Complaint Fails to State Facts Constituting the Enumerated Causes of Action

 

The complaint asserts four causes of action for fraud, one cause of action for perjury, and four derivative intentional infliction of emotional distress claims based on the fraud causes of action.

 

“The elements of fraud, which give rise to the tort action for deceit, are (a) misrepresentation (false representation, concealment, or nondisclosure); (b) knowledge of falsity (or ‘scienter’); (c) intent to defraud, i.e., to induce reliance; (d) justifiable reliance; and (e) resulting damage.” (Lazar v. Superior Court (1996) 12 Cal.4th 631, 638.) The complaint fails to plead all the elements of fraud as the claims are based upon alleged erroneous legal rulings, not any misrepresentation, concealment, or nondisclosure upon which Plaintiff relied to their detriment.

 

“There is no civil cause of action for perjury.” (Pollock v. University of Southern California (2003) 112 Cal.App.4th 1416, 1429.)

 

“A cause of action for intentional infliction of emotional distress exists when there is (1) extreme and outrageous conduct by the defendant with the intention of causing, or reckless disregard of the probability of causing, emotional distress; (2) the plaintiff’s suffering severe or extreme emotional distress; and (3) actual and proximate causation of the emotional distress by the defendant’s outrageous conduct. A defendant’s conduct is outrageous when it is so extreme as to exceed all bounds of that usually tolerated in a civilized community. And the defendant’s conduct must be intended to inflict injury or engaged in with the realization that injury will result.” (Hughes v. Pair (2009) 46 Cal.4th 1035, 1050–1051.) The complaint alleges Defendant made erroneous legal determinations, which is not sufficient to support a claim for intentional infliction of emotional distress.

 

Plaintiff Has Not Demonstrated a Basis for Leave to Amend

 

As noted herein, Plaintiff’s claims against the Defendant, a judicial officer, are barred by judicial immunity The demurrer is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Hon. Carmen Luege’s Demurrer to Plaintiff’s Complaint is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.
