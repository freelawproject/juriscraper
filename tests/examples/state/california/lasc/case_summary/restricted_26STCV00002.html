How can I access my case remotely?
Some cases, such as eviction cases, have restricted access. Only the parties involved in the case, individuals that can provide specific information regarding the case, or those individuals with a court order can access the Court file, including the Register of Actions and document images. To gain access to the case, you must obtain a court-provided access code or give specific information, such as the names of at least one plaintiff and one defendant, and the exact address of the premises including the apartment or unit number, if any. If you are unable to provide this information, you may be required to obtain a court order to access the restricted case file.

Am I required to have an access code?
A court-issued access code is not required if you are able to provide the required information and your responses are successfully validated against the Court’s records.

How can I get an access code?
You may visit the clerk’s office of any courthouse and provide proof of identity (e.g., driver’s license, passport or other government issued pictured identification) to obtain an access code. A list of courthouse locations can be found at https://www.lacourt.org/courthouse.

Does the access code expire?
If you are a party to the case, the access code will not expire. The same code may be used each time you access the case remotely. If you are not a party to the case, the access code will expire in approximately five (5) days after it is generated.

Where can I find the names of the parties and the premises address?
If you are named as a defendant, you can find this information on the Notice of Unlawful Detainer (Eviction) that was mailed to the premises. If you are the plaintiff, you can refer to the Unlawful Detainer Complaint document that you filed to initiate the case.
